Question-level follow-up

Teaching Priorities

Accuracy on seven selected questions

The priority review pairs all five Geometry questions with two quadratic tasks. Darker cells mark lower accuracy and areas to examine first.

What to examine

In Algebra II, 62% answer Q17 on triangle angles correctly; 26% answer Q26 on a constrained circle correctly. These questions make different demands. Use explanations to identify the barrier rather than treating the gap as lost learning.

Q4 asks students to identify a quadratic from a table (63% correct in Algebra II). Q10 asks which equivalent form reveals a minimum (51%). The contrast supports checking how students connect representations and structure.

The follow-up tasks below are proposed checks, not diagnoses inferred from a wrong answer. Confirm content exposure before deciding on reteaching.

Assessment coverage, scoring, and limitations

This administration uses LOGOS-MATH-V2 revision 5: 30 questions, 45 working minutes, a shared clock, and one point per correct response. The online practice assessment uses the identical frozen form and scoring rules.

There are 15,000 response records from 500 students. 97.6% of questions were attempted; 364 were skipped, 1 not reached, and 0 attempted entries invalid. Unanswered and invalid responses earn zero points; there is no penalty for an incorrect answer.

Scores describe performance on these tasks. There are no national norms, SAT conversions, placement cutoffs, equated growth scores, or claims of assessment validity. Enrollment is known; opportunity to learn is unconfirmed. A single administration cannot isolate instruction or teacher effects.

This designed fictional scenario illustrates overlapping track totals, geometric readiness concerns, and class variation. Its responses are synthetic; all scores, comparisons, and figures are calculated from those responses. How to read distributions and uncertainty

Violin plots show estimated score density, boxes the middle half, the line the median, and the white diamond the mean. All students answer the same questions. Density curves are descriptive and can smooth over gaps in discrete scores.

Means are student-weighted. Intervals use student-level t calculations. The 22 planned comparisons use Welch intervals and Holm adjustment within three separate families: 14 class pairs within the same course and track, four track contrasts, and four adjacent-course contrasts. Class tests are adjusted across all 14 pairs, not separately by course. The 45-pair matrix is a separate exploratory family. Intervals are pointwise, not simultaneous.

The illustrative equivalence margin is ±5 percentage points, not an established educational standard. A 90% interval must fall wholly inside that margin; an inconclusive difference does not establish equivalence. No multiplicity-adjusted equivalence claim is made.

The participants are not a random sample of schools. Statistical calculations show uncertainty under the stated model; they cannot justify generalizing to a population or treating this illustration as validation.
